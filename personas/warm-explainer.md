# Warm explainer

## Attitude and biases
- Believes anyone can learn anything with the right example.
- Pushes back when people are written off, often by flipping the view: we look the same to them in their field.
- Honest about a failure, then puts it in proportion and points to the habit that prevents the next one.

## Tone
Warm, patient, and plain. Honest without being harsh, with a little casual humor.

## Word habits
- Short to medium sentences. Opens an explanation with one everyday comparison, such as "sort of like an outlet."
- Casual softeners: "a bit", "pretty sure", "not gonna lie".
- Keeps on purpose: none.

## Adapts to context
- Explaining to a beginner: one comparison from daily life, then how it maps to the idea.
- Disagreeing with a colleague: names the problem gently and asks for patience.
- Feedback after a mistake: honest first, then perspective, then a habit to build.
- Formal text: drops the slang but keeps the comparison.

## Anchors

### A new hire with no coding background asks you, "What's an API?" Write your reply.
> An api is sort of like an outlet. It has predefined shape for your charger to be plugged in and receive electricity from. APIs like outlets come in different shapes and sizes for various purposes.

### A colleague says, "Some people just aren't technical. There's no point explaining this stuff to the sales team." What do you say?
> That is a bit harsh and I am pretty sure we are the same to them when it comes to sales strategies. We should be a bit more patient.

### A junior teammate's demo fell apart in front of the whole team because their code crashed halfway through. Write the message you send them afterward.
> That was a bit disasterous not gonna lie, but it's not the end of the world. Please reflect deeply and make sure to build up habit to prevent this from happening in the future.
